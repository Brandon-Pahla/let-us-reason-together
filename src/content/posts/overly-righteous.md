<section id="intro">
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>Do not be overly righteous,<br>Nor be overly wise:<br>Why should you destroy yourself?</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ecclesiastes%207%3A16&amp;version=NKJV">Ecclesiastes 7:16</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p class="note">In the scripture quotations, the words of Jesus are in red.</p>
<p>Some Christians read this verse as a warning against being too zealous. Picture someone newly baptized who feels moved to give all their money to God. That, they say, is exactly what being overly righteous looks like.</p>
<p><strong>My answer is no. Giving <span class="accent">everything</span> to God out of love is not, on its own, what Solomon was warning about.</strong></p>
<p>That is a big claim, so the argument has to stand on scripture. The rest of this post works through it, one objection at a time.</p>
</section>

<section id="meaning">
<h2>What does "overly righteous" really mean?</h2>
<p>Here is the passage in full:</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>Do not be overly righteous,<br>Nor be overly wise:<br>Why should you destroy yourself?</p>
<p>Do not be overly wicked,<br>Nor be foolish:<br>Why should you die before your time?</p>
<p><em>It is</em> good that you grasp this,<br>And also not remove your hand from the other;<br>For he who fears God will escape them all.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ecclesiastes%207%3A16-18&amp;version=NKJV">Ecclesiastes 7:16–18</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>Notice what verse 16 pairs it with: "<span class="sq">overly wise</span>." Solomon isn't talking about someone who loves God too much. He is talking about someone who thinks they have climbed above everyone else, in righteousness or in wisdom.</p>
<p>That is self-righteousness. It is spiritual pride, the kind that stacks its own rules on top of God's and then looks down on anyone who doesn't keep them. It is rigid legalism dressed up as devotion.</p>
<p>And look at how the passage ends: "<span class="sq">he who fears God will escape them all</span>." The cure for both extremes is not less devotion. It is the fear of God.</p>
<p>So the warning is about the heart, not the size of the gift. Nothing here says you can give too generously or love God too much.</p>

<h3>What about being too zealous?</h3>
<p>Some put it another way: the overly righteous person is simply too zealous. But Scripture never treats zeal for God as the problem. Christ gave Himself to make a people zealous:</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>who gave Himself for us, that He might redeem us from every lawless deed and purify for Himself <em>His</em> own special people, zealous for good works.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Titus%202%3A14&amp;version=NKJV">Titus 2:14</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>And to a lukewarm church, Jesus says the opposite of "calm down":</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p><span class="wj">“I know your works, that you are neither cold nor hot. I could wish you were cold or hot. So then, because you are lukewarm, and neither cold nor hot, I will vomit you out of My mouth.</span></p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Revelation%203%3A15-16&amp;version=NKJV">Revelation 3:15–16</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p><span class="wj">As many as I love, I rebuke and chasten. Therefore be zealous and repent.</span></p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Revelation%203%3A19&amp;version=NKJV">Revelation 3:19</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>The Bible does warn about one kind of zeal. Paul describes it in his own people:</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>For I bear them witness that they have a zeal for God, but not according to knowledge. For they being ignorant of God’s righteousness, and seeking to establish their own righteousness, have not submitted to the righteousness of God.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%2010%3A2-3&amp;version=NKJV">Romans 10:2–3</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>That is the zeal to watch out for. It isn't too much love for God. It is zeal "<span class="sq">not according to knowledge</span>," spent on establishing "<span class="sq">their own righteousness</span>."</p>
<p>Paul knew it from the inside. Before he met Christ, he was "<span class="sq">more exceedingly zealous for the traditions of my fathers</span>" (<a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Galatians%201%3A14&amp;version=NKJV">Galatians 1:14</a>), and he describes himself as "<span class="sq">concerning zeal, persecuting the church</span>" (<a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Philippians%203%3A6&amp;version=NKJV">Philippians 3:6</a>). Then he adds, "<span class="sq">But what things were gain to me, these I have counted loss for Christ</span>" (<a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Philippians%203%3A7&amp;version=NKJV">Philippians 3:7</a>).</p>
<p>That is what overly righteous looks like: zeal for a righteousness of your own. A new believer giving everything to God out of love shows the other kind, the zeal Christ gave Himself to create.</p>
</section>

<section id="first-widow">
<h2>Did Jesus ever praise giving everything?</h2>
<p>He did. Watch what happens at the temple treasury.</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>Now Jesus sat opposite the treasury and saw how the people put money into the treasury. And many <em>who were</em> rich put in much. Then one poor widow came and threw in two mites, which make a quadrans. So He called His disciples to <em>Himself</em> and said to them, <span class="wj">“Assuredly, I say to you that this poor widow has put in more than all those who have given to the treasury; for they all put in out of their abundance, but she out of her poverty put in all that she had, her whole livelihood.”</span></p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%2012%3A41-44&amp;version=NKJV">Mark 12:41–44</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>She put in "<span class="sq wj">all that she had, her whole livelihood</span>." If giving everything were overly righteous, she would be the textbook case. Jesus said she gave more than everyone else.</p>
<p>Look at the details too. Mark mentions no children and no one else living off those two coins. And she made no announcement. She just dropped them in, and it was Jesus who pointed her out.</p>
<p>If giving everything were spiritual excess, this was the moment for Jesus to say so. He praised her instead.</p>
</section>

<section id="second-widow">
<h2>Wasn't the widow of Zarephath just following orders?</h2>
<p>The Old Testament has a second widow who gave everything. Elijah meets her at the city gate during the drought:</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>So he arose and went to Zarephath. And when he came to the gate of the city, indeed a widow <em>was</em> there gathering sticks. And he called to her and said, “Please bring me a little water in a cup, that I may drink.” And as she was going to get <em>it,</em> he called to her and said, “Please bring me a morsel of bread in your hand.”</p>
<p>So she said, “As the <span class="sc">Lord</span> your God lives, I do not have bread, only a handful of flour in a bin, and a little oil in a jar; and see, I <em>am</em> gathering a couple of sticks that I may go in and prepare it for myself and my son, that we may eat it, and die.”</p>
<p>And Elijah said to her, “Do not fear; go <em>and</em> do as you have said, but make me a small cake from it first, and bring <em>it</em> to me; and afterward make <em>some</em> for yourself and your son. For thus says the <span class="sc">Lord</span> God of Israel: ‘The bin of flour shall not be used up, nor shall the jar of oil run dry, until the day the <span class="sc">Lord</span> sends rain on the earth.’ ”</p>
<p>So she went away and did according to the word of Elijah; and she and he and her household ate for <em>many</em> days. The bin of flour was not used up, nor did the jar of oil run dry, according to the word of the <span class="sc">Lord</span> which He spoke by Elijah.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%2017%3A10-16&amp;version=NKJV">1 Kings 17:10–16</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>A common reply is that her giving doesn't count. She was obeying a direct command from Elijah, so she can't have been overly righteous.</p>
<p>I don't think that holds up. She didn't know Elijah. He was a stranger at the gate, and she lived in Zarephath, which belongs to Sidon (<a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%2017%3A9&amp;version=NKJV">1 Kings 17:9</a>).</p>
<p>She could tell he worshipped the <span class="sc">Lord</span>, because she says "<span class="sq">As the <span class="sc">Lord</span> your God lives</span>." But she had no proof that this man really spoke for Him.</p>
<p>Anyone can walk up and say "thus says the <span class="sc">Lord</span>." A false command can come from anywhere. A word from a stranger is only worth obeying if you trust the God behind it.</p>
<p>That is exactly what she did. She doesn't say "<span class="sq">Now by this I know that you <em>are</em> a man of God</span>" until later, after Elijah raises her son (<a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%2017%3A24&amp;version=NKJV">1 Kings 17:24</a>). When she baked that first small cake, she had nothing to go on but faith. <strong>Her obedience was righteous because of her faith, not because someone gave her an order.</strong></p>
<p>Someone might point to verse 9, where God tells Elijah, "<span class="sq">See, I have commanded a widow there to provide for you</span>" (<a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%2017%3A9&amp;version=NKJV">1 Kings 17:9</a>). But the text never says she heard that command herself. What she heard was a stranger asking for her last meal, and she still had to trust God enough to act.</p>
<p>So how did God command her? I believe it was a conviction in her heart. That is how God directs His people: "<span class="sq">it is God who works in you both to will and to do for <em>His</em> good pleasure</span>" (<a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Philippians%202%3A13&amp;version=NKJV">Philippians 2:13</a>).</p>
<p>And that works for anyone today. If God convicts someone to give everything they have to the poor, that is God commanding them. By the objection's own logic, obeying God's command can't be overly righteous.</p>
<p>Jesus gave exactly that command to the man often called the rich young ruler:</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>Jesus said to him, <span class="wj">“If you want to be perfect, go, sell what you have and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow Me.”</span></p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%2019%3A21&amp;version=NKJV">Matthew 19:21</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>Here Jesus gave that command to one man, not to a crowd. But if He gave it to anyone, giving everything cannot be what Solomon was warning against.</p>
<p>And don't miss this: she had a son. She gave away her last meal while someone depended on her. God didn't let either of them starve.</p>
</section>

<section id="family">
<h2>"But what about the family?"</h2>
<p>This is the strongest objection. Giving everything, some say, becomes overly righteous when it hurts your family.</p>
<p>I agree, up to a point. The Bible takes family seriously, and no one should leave their dependents with nothing. But I have two reservations.</p>

<h3>If no one depends on you</h3>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p><span class="wj">“Take heed that you do not do your charitable deeds before men, to be seen by them. Otherwise you have no reward from your Father in heaven. Therefore, when you do a charitable deed, do not sound a trumpet before you as the hypocrites do in the synagogues and in the streets, that they may have glory from men. Assuredly, I say to you, they have their reward. But when you do a charitable deed, do not let your left hand know what your right hand is doing, that your charitable deed may be in secret; and your Father who sees in secret will Himself reward you openly.</span></p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%206%3A1-4&amp;version=NKJV">Matthew 6:1–4</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>Say nobody depends on you. You give in secret, so no one knows. You do it out of real love for God, to move the gospel forward.</p>
<p>What exactly is overly righteous about that? There is no pride in it, and there is no audience. That is pure devotion, the same as the poor widow's.</p>

<h3>If you do have a family</h3>
<p>Now say you do have a family. Giving away everything you currently have is still not a death sentence. You are still alive, and you can still work.</p>
<p>Scripture is clear that providing for your household is your job:</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>But if anyone does not provide for his own, and especially for those of his household, he has denied the faith and is worse than an unbeliever.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Timothy%205%3A8&amp;version=NKJV">1 Timothy 5:8</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>For even when we were with you, we commanded you this: If anyone will not work, neither shall he eat. For we hear that there are some who walk among you in a disorderly manner, not working at all, but are busybodies. Now those who are such we command and exhort through our Lord Jesus Christ that they work in quietness and eat their own bread.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Thessalonians%203%3A10-12&amp;version=NKJV">2 Thessalonians 3:10–12</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>Notice what Paul condemns. It isn't giving. It's refusing to work. Someone who gives away their savings and keeps working to feed their family is doing exactly what these verses ask.</p>
<p>And your paycheck isn't the whole picture. Jesus told us who holds the rest:</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p><span class="wj">“Therefore do not worry, saying, ‘What shall we eat?’ or ‘What shall we drink?’ or ‘What shall we wear?’ For after all these things the Gentiles seek. For your heavenly Father knows that you need all these things. But seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness, and all these things shall be added to you.</span></p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%206%3A31-33&amp;version=NKJV">Matthew 6:31–33</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>And God <em>is</em> able to make all grace abound toward you, that you, always having all sufficiency in all <em>things,</em> may have an abundance for every good work. As it is written:</p>
<p>“He has dispersed abroad,<br>He has given to the poor;<br>His righteousness endures forever.”</p>
<p>Now may He who supplies seed to the sower, and bread for food, supply and multiply the seed you have <em>sown</em> and increase the fruits of your righteousness,</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Corinthians%209%3A8-10&amp;version=NKJV">2 Corinthians 9:8–10</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>

<h3>How the disciples did it</h3>
<p>The disciples lived this out. James and John walked away from the family fishing business, but the business was still there. Zebedee stayed in the boat with the hired servants:</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>When He had gone a little farther from there, He saw James the <em>son</em> of Zebedee, and John his brother, who also <em>were</em> in the boat mending their nets. And immediately He called them, and they left their father Zebedee in the boat with the hired servants, and went after Him.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%201%3A19-20&amp;version=NKJV">Mark 1:19–20</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>Simon and Andrew still had their house. Simon had a wife and a mother-in-law, and Jesus walked into that home and healed her:</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>Now as soon as they had come out of the synagogue, they entered the house of Simon and Andrew, with James and John. But Simon’s wife’s mother lay sick with a fever, and they told Him about her at once. So He came and took her by the hand and lifted her up, and immediately the fever left her. And she served them.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%201%3A29-31&amp;version=NKJV">Mark 1:29–31</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>After the resurrection, Peter still had his trade, and he went back out on the water with the others:</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>Simon Peter, Thomas called the Twin, Nathanael of Cana in Galilee, the <em>sons</em> of Zebedee, and two others of His disciples were together. Simon Peter said to them, “I am going fishing.”</p>
<p>They said to him, “We are going with you also.” They went out and immediately got into the boat, and that night they caught nothing.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2021%3A2-3&amp;version=NKJV">John 21:2–3</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>While they travelled with Jesus, God provided through people who gave:</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>…and Joanna the wife of Chuza, Herod’s steward, and Susanna, and many others who provided for Him from their substance.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%208%3A3&amp;version=NKJV">Luke 8:3</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>And on top of all that, Jesus made a promise to everyone who gives things up for Him:</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p><span class="wj">And everyone who has left houses or brothers or sisters or father or mother or wife or children or lands, for My name’s sake, shall receive a hundredfold, and inherit eternal life.</span></p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%2019%3A29&amp;version=NKJV">Matthew 19:29</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>So here is the real difference. <strong>Giving away stored wealth while you keep working doesn't take away your family's security. It moves that security from a bank balance to God.</strong></p>
</section>

<section id="neighbor">
<h2>Doesn't "love your neighbor as yourself" set a limit?</h2>
<p>Another objection goes like this. God says to love your neighbor as yourself. You would never give your neighbor everything and keep nothing for yourself, so giving everything means loving others more than yourself.</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>You shall not take vengeance, nor bear any grudge against the children of your people, but you shall love your neighbor as yourself: I <em>am</em> the <span class="sc">Lord</span>.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Leviticus%2019%3A18&amp;version=NKJV">Leviticus 19:18</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>I don't think "as yourself" was ever meant to be a ceiling. Jesus raised the bar Himself:</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p><span class="wj">A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another; as I have loved you, that you also love one another.</span></p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2013%3A34&amp;version=NKJV">John 13:34</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p><span class="wj">This is My commandment, that you love one another as I have loved you. Greater love has no one than this, than to lay down one’s life for his friends.</span></p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2015%3A12-13&amp;version=NKJV">John 15:12–13</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>Not "as you love yourself," but "<span class="sq wj">as I have loved you</span>." And Jesus did not love us with a careful, keep-something-back kind of love:</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though He was rich, yet for your sakes He became poor, that you through His poverty might become rich.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Corinthians%208%3A9&amp;version=NKJV">2 Corinthians 8:9</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>He was rich, and He became poor for us. If loving others more than yourself were a fault, Jesus could never have done it.</p>

<h3>The early church did it too</h3>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>Moreover, brethren, we make known to you the grace of God bestowed on the churches of Macedonia: that in a great trial of affliction the abundance of their joy and their deep poverty abounded in the riches of their liberality. For I bear witness that according to <em>their</em> ability, yes, and beyond <em>their</em> ability, <em>they were</em> freely willing, imploring us with much urgency that we would receive the gift and the fellowship of the ministering to the saints. And not <em>only</em> as we had hoped, but they first gave themselves to the Lord, and <em>then</em> to us by the will of God.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Corinthians%208%3A1-5&amp;version=NKJV">2 Corinthians 8:1–5</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>The Macedonians were poor, and they gave beyond their ability. Notice the order in verse 5: "<span class="sq">they first gave themselves to the Lord</span>." Their giving to people grew out of giving themselves to God first.</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>Now the multitude of those who believed were of one heart and one soul; neither did anyone say that any of the things he possessed was his own, but they had all things in common. And with great power the apostles gave witness to the resurrection of the Lord Jesus. And great grace was upon them all. Nor was there anyone among them who lacked; for all who were possessors of lands or houses sold them, and brought the proceeds of the things that were sold, and laid <em>them</em> at the apostles’ feet; and they distributed to each as anyone had need.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts%204%3A32-35&amp;version=NKJV">Acts 4:32–35</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>Nobody in that church kept a careful balance between loving themselves and loving others. And look at the result: "<span class="sq">Nor was there anyone among them who lacked</span>." Giving everything didn't leave them destitute. It left a church where no one went without.</p>

<h3>"But God doesn't want you to suffer"</h3>
<p>Some add one more thing. God doesn't want you to suffer, they say, so don't put yourself in that position.</p>
<p>First, giving everything is not the same as suffering. We have already seen that God provides for those who seek His kingdom first (<a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%206%3A33&amp;version=NKJV">Matthew 6:33</a>). And in the church that gave everything, "<span class="sq">Nor was there anyone among them who lacked</span>" (<a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts%204%3A34&amp;version=NKJV">Acts 4:34</a>).</p>
<p>Second, Scripture never makes avoiding hardship the goal. Moses could have kept his place as the son of Pharaoh’s daughter, and he chose otherwise:</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>By faith Moses, when he became of age, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh’s daughter, choosing rather to suffer affliction with the people of God than to enjoy the passing pleasures of sin, esteeming the reproach of Christ greater riches than the treasures in Egypt; for he looked to the reward.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews%2011%3A24-26&amp;version=NKJV">Hebrews 11:24–26</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>He chose that path because "<span class="sq">he looked to the reward</span>." The early believers showed the same outlook when their goods were taken:</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>for you had compassion on me in my chains, and joyfully accepted the plundering of your goods, knowing that you have a better and an enduring possession for yourselves in heaven.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews%2010%3A34&amp;version=NKJV">Hebrews 10:34</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>Third, the New Testament treats suffering for Christ as a gift, not a mistake:</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>For to you it has been granted on behalf of Christ, not only to believe in Him, but also to suffer for His sake,</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Philippians%201%3A29&amp;version=NKJV">Philippians 1:29</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>Beloved, do not think it strange concerning the fiery trial which is to try you, as though some strange thing happened to you; but rejoice to the extent that you partake of Christ’s sufferings, that when His glory is revealed, you may also be glad with exceeding joy.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Peter%204%3A12-13&amp;version=NKJV">1 Peter 4:12–13</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>That doesn't mean we go looking for pain. It means that when following Christ costs us something, the cost is no sign we've gone wrong. Paul says plainly that "<span class="sq">all who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution</span>" (<a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Timothy%203%3A12&amp;version=NKJV">2 Timothy 3:12</a>).</p>
<p>Fourth, Paul shows where our security really sits, whatever comes:</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>Not that I speak in regard to need, for I have learned in whatever state I am, to be content: I know how to be abased, and I know how to abound. Everywhere and in all things I have learned both to be full and to be hungry, both to abound and to suffer need. I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Philippians%204%3A11-13&amp;version=NKJV">Philippians 4:11–13</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>The goal was never a life without need. It was contentment in Christ, whether full or hungry.</p>
</section>

<section id="pharisees">
<h2>Weren't the Pharisees righteous?</h2>
<p>Behind the "overly righteous" label is a picture of the Pharisees as people who were too righteous. I don't think they were righteous at all. They were only righteous in their own eyes, and Jesus said as much:</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>And He said to them, <span class="wj">“You are those who justify yourselves before men, but God knows your hearts. For what is highly esteemed among men is an abomination in the sight of God.</span></p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%2016%3A15&amp;version=NKJV">Luke 16:15</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p>Also He spoke this parable to some who trusted in themselves that they were righteous, and despised others:</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%2018%3A9&amp;version=NKJV">Luke 18:9</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p><span class="wj">“Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you are like whitewashed tombs which indeed appear beautiful outwardly, but inside are full of dead <em>men’s</em> bones and all uncleanness. Even so you also outwardly appear righteous to men, but inside you are full of hypocrisy and lawlessness.</span></p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%2023%3A27-28&amp;version=NKJV">Matthew 23:27–28</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>That can sound unbiblical at first. After all, Jesus used the Pharisees as the benchmark:</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p><span class="wj">For I say to you, that unless your righteousness exceeds <em>the righteousness</em> of the scribes and Pharisees, you will by no means enter the kingdom of heaven.</span></p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%205%3A20&amp;version=NKJV">Matthew 5:20</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>But think about what that verse does to the "overly righteous" argument. You can't warn someone against being "overly righteous" and then quote a verse that commands us to be more righteous than the Pharisees. If we must exceed them, the danger was never too much righteousness.</p>
<p>The answer is that the Pharisees had the wrong kind. Theirs was outward, self-made, and on display, and before God it counted for nothing. Jesus wasn't asking for more of what they had. <strong>He was calling for a completely different righteousness, one that grows out of a surrendered heart.</strong></p>
<p>That, I think, is the real face of "overly righteous." It looks like the Pharisee in the parable who "<span class="sq">stood and prayed thus with himself</span>" (<a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%2018%3A11&amp;version=NKJV">Luke 18:11</a>). It doesn't look like a widow quietly giving everything she had.</p>
</section>

<section id="egw">
<h2>What Ellen G. White says on the issue</h2>
<p>Ellen G. White wrote on both halves of this question, so here is what she says. I give the reference codes and links so you can check each passage in context.</p>
<p>Ellen White quoted the King James Version, so the scripture wording inside her quotes differs.</p>

<h3>On Matthew 5:20</h3>
<p>On what Jesus exposed in the Pharisees' righteousness:</p>
<blockquote class="egw">
<p>Jesus unveiled the deception. He declared that the righteousness upon which the Pharisees set so great value was worthless. … All their pretensions of piety, their human inventions and ceremonies, and even their boasted performance of the outward requirements of the law, could not avail to make them holy. They were not pure in heart or noble and Christlike in character.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://m.egwwritings.org/en/book/150.278">MB 53.1</a> (Thoughts From the Mount of Blessing, ch. 3, "The Spirituality of the Law")</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>On where that righteousness came from:</p>
<blockquote class="egw">
<p>In their professed service to God, the Jews were really working for self. Their righteousness was the fruit of their own efforts to keep the law according to their own ideas and for their own selfish benefit. Hence it could be no better than they were.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://m.egwwritings.org/en/book/150.283">MB 54.1</a> (Thoughts From the Mount of Blessing, ch. 3, "The Spirituality of the Law")</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>On how God saw it:</p>
<blockquote class="egw">
<p>External forms cannot take the place of inward piety. … But with all their criticisms and exactions, with all their forms and ceremonies, they were an offense to God.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://m.egwwritings.org/en/book/126.734">TMK 114.3</a> (That I May Know Him, April 18, "The Righteousness That God Requires")</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>And on the righteousness Jesus was actually asking for:</p>
<blockquote class="egw">
<p>The disciples of Christ must obtain righteousness of a different character from that of the Pharisees, if they would enter the kingdom of heaven. God offered them, in His Son, the perfect righteousness of the law.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://m.egwwritings.org/en/book/150.284">MB 54.2</a> (Thoughts From the Mount of Blessing, ch. 3, "The Spirituality of the Law")</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>That is the contradiction in a nutshell. <strong>The Pharisees' righteousness was not too much of a good thing. It was worthless, because it never touched the heart.</strong></p>

<h3>On the widow's gift</h3>
<blockquote class="egw">
<p>But Jesus understood her motive. … Her heart went with her gift; its value was estimated, not by the worth of the coin, but by the love to God and the interest in His work that had prompted the deed.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://m.egwwritings.org/en/book/130.3013">DA 615.1</a> (The Desire of Ages, ch. 67, "Woes on the Pharisees")</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<blockquote class="egw">
<p>He esteemed her gift of more value than the large offerings of those whose alms did not call for self-denial. … Thus He taught that the value of the gift is estimated not by the amount, but by the proportion that is given and the motive that actuates the giver.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://m.egwwritings.org/en/book/127.1510">AA 342.1</a> (The Acts of the Apostles, ch. 32, "A Liberal Church")</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>Total, self-denying sacrifice, prompted by love for God, is exactly what she holds up as the standard here.</p>

<h3>On caring for your family</h3>
<p>She also keeps the duty to your household firmly in place. In counsel about leaving property by will, she writes:</p>
<blockquote class="egw">
<p>In disposing of your property by will to your relatives, be sure that you do not forget God's cause. … Your wife and children, of course, should not be left destitute; provision should be made for them if they are needy.</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://m.egwwritings.org/en/book/22.1897">CS 328.2</a> (Counsels on Stewardship)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>And writing to someone who was idle and running into debt for his family's provisions, she points straight to work:</p>
<blockquote class="egw">
<p>The very best sermon you could preach to the world would be to show a decided reformation in your life, and provide for your own family. Says the apostle, “If any provide not for his own, and specially for those of his own house, he hath denied the faith, and is worse than an infidel.”</p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://m.egwwritings.org/en/book/22.1478">CS 253.1</a> (Counsels on Stewardship)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
<p>Put those together and you get the same balance Scripture gives. Don't leave your dependents destitute, and keep working to provide for them. But when someone with no dependents gives quietly out of love, or someone keeps working faithfully for their family while giving their stored wealth to God, nothing here calls that excess. It is the widow's kind of gift.</p>
</section>

<section id="conclusion">
<h2>So who is really being overly righteous?</h2>
<p>Here is the question I keep coming back to.</p>
<p>When a new believer wants to give everything to God, why is our first instinct to call it too much? Jesus praised that exact act. The God of Elijah honoured it. The disciples lived it.</p>
<p>Maybe the label isn't about protecting them. Maybe it's about protecting us. If radical faith is "overly righteous," then our comfort is safe, our savings are safe, and we never have to ask where our trust really sits.</p>
<p>So be honest with yourself. When you hear about someone giving it all away, is your problem with their righteousness, or with what it shows you about your own?</p>
<blockquote class="scripture">
<p><span class="wj">But seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness, and all these things shall be added to you.</span></p>
<footer><cite><a href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%206%3A33&amp;version=NKJV">Matthew 6:33</a> (NKJV)</cite></footer>
</blockquote>
</section>
